Overview

Y Combinator's Winter 2026 (W26) batch represents the accelerator's continued expansion under its four-batches-per-year cadence, introduced in 2025. The W26 cohort includes approximately 120 companies drawn from a global applicant pool, with Demo Day scheduled for March 24, 2026 at the Computer History Museum in San Francisco. [s1] The batch will present to an invite-only audience of roughly 1,500 investors and media. [s2]

Under the standard YC deal, each company receives $500,000 in exchange for 7% equity, structured as two SAFE agreements: a fixed $125,000 component converting to a 7% stake and a variable $375,000 component that converts based on the next funding round's valuation. [s3] With the shift to four annual cohorts, each batch is smaller and more selective than the 250–300-company mega-batches of 2023–2024, though Y Combinator's total annual throughput remains roughly constant. [s3] Starting with the Spring 2026 batch, YC will also offer founders the option to receive their funding in USDC stablecoins on Ethereum, Base, or Solana — a first among legacy accelerators. [s10] YC also published its full 2026 Demo Day calendar: W26 on March 24, Spring on June 16, Summer on September 10, and Fall on December 2. [s1]

The W26 batch is heavily weighted toward AI and automation, continuing a trend that has seen over 50% of recent YC companies incorporate AI into their core product. Agentic AI — systems that autonomously perform tasks rather than merely assisting human decision-making — is the dominant theme, appearing across sectors from healthcare to defense to supply chain logistics. [s4] YC's 2026 Request for Startups reinforces this direction, calling for AI-native companies across ten focus areas including product management AI, government modernization, spatial reasoning models, and AI-guided physical work. [s11]

The W26 cohort is also benefiting from an expanded mentorship bench: nine new Visiting Partners — all YC alumni founders — joined in early 2026, including Harshita Arora (AtoB, S20), Raphael Schaad (Cron, W20, acquired by Notion), and Grey Baker (Dependabot, acquired by GitHub). [s12] In a separate policy shift, YC reversed a November 2025 decision to exclude Canadian incorporations, reinstating Canada alongside the US, Cayman Islands, and Singapore as accepted jurisdictions. [s13]

Batch Statistics

Batch Size & Selection

The W26 batch comprises approximately 120 companies based on the YC directory, roughly half the size of 2023–2024 era batches when YC ran only two cohorts per year. The acceptance rate for recent YC batches has hovered around 1–2%, making it one of the most selective startup programs globally. [s5] Applications for W26 closed on November 10, 2025. [s6] The batch is overseen by a growing roster of YC partners including Brad Flora, Gustaf Alstromer, Jared Friedman, Tyler Bosmeny, Harshita Arora, Ankit Gupta, Diana Hu, Jon Xu, Aaron Epstein, and Joshua Browder. [s7] [s12]

Geographic Distribution

While the majority of W26 companies are US-based, the batch includes international founders from the Netherlands (Servo7, Patientdesk.ai), Ireland (Squid Energy), the United Kingdom (Seeing Systems), and Australia (GrazeMate), reflecting YC's growing global reach. India continues to be a significant source of YC founders. [s7]

Founding Stage

The median founding year for W26 companies is 2025, with a handful founded in 2026 (Sequence Markets, Scout Out, Hex Security, MouseCat). A few more mature companies also appear — Balance (founded 2019), Jinba (2023), and Pocket (with roots dating to 1995) — indicating YC's willingness to back companies at various stages. [s7]

Notable Companies

Several W26 companies stand out for their ambition, traction, or funding:

Hex Security

Hex Security (hex.co) — "Agentic Offensive Security at Scale." The cybersecurity company has raised $172 million, making it by far the best-funded company in the W26 batch. With 201–500 employees and a focus on agentic penetration testing, Hex represents a later-stage entry into YC. Founded 2026; YC partner: Gustaf Alstromer. [s7]

Sequence Markets

Sequence Markets (sequencemkts.com) — "Smarter and faster trade execution for digital assets." Having raised $20 million, the crypto trading infrastructure company is the second-best-funded in the batch. Founded by Muhammad Awan, Peter Bai, and Frank Zou; YC partner: Brad Flora. [s7]

Constellation Space Corp

Constellation Space Corp (constellation-io.com) — "AI for space mission assurance." This space-tech startup ranked #1 on the Best of Show HN list for YC W26, generating significant developer community interest. YC partner: Jared Friedman. [s8]

HLabs

HLabs (hlaboratories.com) — "Developer-friendly robotics." Founded by Paul Hetherington (a W21 alumnus and former CEO of Mystic), HLabs provides modular robotic components — described as "plug-and-play organs for machines." Over 40 robotics companies adopted its actuator controller before full launch. YC partner: Brad Flora. [s9]

GRU Space

GRU Space (gru.space) — "Building the First Hotel on the Moon." Perhaps the most audacious company in the batch, GRU Space is working on lunar hospitality infrastructure. YC partner: Brad Flora. [s7]

ARC Prize Foundation

ARC Prize Foundation (arcprize.org) — "AI benchmarks that measure general intelligence." A nonprofit focused on advancing AGI research through public competitions and benchmarks. YC partner: Tyler Bosmeny. [s7]

LunaBill

LunaBill (lunabill.com) — "AI Voice Callers for Healthcare Billing Teams." LunaBill's voice agents, fine-tuned on a dataset of over 1.2 million call transcripts, automate insurance calls for claim-status inquiries, A/R recovery, and appeals follow-up. The company has automated 60,000+ insurance calls, saved billing teams over 20,000 hours, and recovered more than $30 million in payments. It reports $764K in contracted ARR since launching in July 2025. [s14]

Chamber

Chamber (chamber.ai) — "AI Infrastructure Autopilot." Founded by Charles Ding alongside fellow Amazon veterans Shaocheng Wang, Jason Ong, and Andreas Bloomquist, Chamber enables companies to run on average 50% more workloads on existing AI infrastructure without adding hardware or headcount. [s15]

Corvera

Corvera (corvera.ai) — "Fully Autonomous Operations for CPG Brands." Founded by Christopher Kong (2x founder, ex-CEO of Better Nature, Forbes 30U30), Dirk Breeuwer (ex-Google Data & AI Lead), and Matthew Collins (ex-CPO at Rosemark, Princeton MEng CS), Corvera automates sales order processing, inventory management, and demand forecasting for consumer packaged goods brands. The company raised $2M in its first month full-time and has secured 15 design partners with a pipeline of 30+ brands. [s17] [s18]

Beacon Health

Beacon Health (beaconhealth.ai) — "AI Agents for Primary Care." Beacon Health deploys computer-usage AI agents that log directly into electronic health records to complete tasks on behalf of physicians — quality gap closures, risk adjustment, prior authorizations, referrals, and patient outreach. The company targets value-based care organizations including independent practices, IPAs, and ACOs. [s19]

Funding & Exits

As of mid-February 2026, most W26 companies have not yet completed their post-Demo Day fundraising rounds, since Demo Day is not until March 24. However, several companies entered YC with existing funding: [s7]

Hex Security stands out with $172 million in funding, though this appears to be a later-stage company joining YC for strategic rather than capital reasons. Sequence Markets has raised $20 million. Other notable raises include Chasi AI ($2.5M), Corvera ($2M in its first month, led by Firstminute Capital and angel investors), GrazeMate ($1.2M), Caretta ($1.08M), DroneTector ($617K), and several companies with $500K rounds (Veriad, Bubble Lab, Patientdesk.ai, Pax Historia, Luel, Skillsync, Laurence, o11). [s7] [s17] [s18]

Under YC's current terms, each company receives $500,000 for 7% equity. The effective dilution varies based on subsequent fundraising: at a $10M pre-money seed, YC's total ownership comes to approximately 10.75%, while at a $20M pre-money, it falls to roughly 8.9%. Including living expenses and legal costs during the three-month program, founders should budget $52,000–$130,000 in total costs per founder. [s3]

In a significant structural change, YC announced on February 3, 2026 that it will offer founders the option to receive their $500,000 allocation in Circle-issued USDC stablecoins, available on Ethereum, Base, and Solana. Visiting Partner Nemil Dalal, who leads YC's crypto focus, stated: "Stablecoins is one of the key pillars for us. So we just want to live and breathe that as well." The stablecoin option takes effect with the Spring 2026 batch but signals YC's deepening commitment to crypto infrastructure. [s10] Separately, YC partnered with Coinbase and Base to launch a "Fintech 3.0" initiative and Request for Startups focused on onchain financial services, citing the passage of the GENIUS Act as creating a clear US crypto regulatory framework. [s16]

Post-Demo Day fundraising data will become available in Q2 2026. Historically, top-performing YC companies raise seed rounds of $2–5 million within weeks of Demo Day, with some high-profile companies raising larger rounds. The current AI funding boom suggests strong investor appetite for the batch's AI-heavy composition. [s4]
