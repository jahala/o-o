AGI Declared

As of — • Version 0

Status

As of 11 November 2026, no major AI company or research lab has formally declared that it has achieved Artificial General Intelligence. The closest event so far came on 3 September 2026, when OpenAI launched GPT-6 Astra and its president, Greg Brockman, told reporters that "it's not unreasonable to feel that we are now in the AGI era" [s20]. That was a personal view stated at a press briefing. OpenAI did not issue a formal declaration, and the ARC Prize Foundation, which tested the model, wrote that "we are not claiming that it is AGI" [s21].

OpenAI's leaders have said a declaration may come soon. In a TIME interview published on 26 August 2026, CEO Sam Altman said OpenAI was "not quite yet" at AGI, but that by the end of the year the company would have an internal system he would call AGI. Chief research officer Mark Chen estimated the company was "80% of the way" there [s23]. No such announcement has been made as of this update.

Informal claims from outside the labs have grown louder. In March 2026, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said on Lex Fridman's podcast "I think it's now" and that AGI had been reached. He used a narrow definition proposed by Fridman: an AI that could start and grow a technology company worth $1 billion [s25]. Huang repeated the claim after the Astra launch, with little market reaction [s26]. Earlier claims include Altman's late-2025 remark that "AGI kinda went whooshing by" [s1], a February 2026 Nature commentary arguing that human-level machine intelligence "is now a reality" [s2], and Sequoia Capital's January 2026 essay "2026: This is AGI" [s12]. None of these is a declaration by a lab about its own system.

A formal declaration also carries less legal weight than it did. Under the October 2025 Microsoft–OpenAI agreement, any AGI declaration by OpenAI had to be verified by an independent expert panel [s3]. On 27 April 2026 the two companies amended the deal: Microsoft's license became non-exclusive, and OpenAI's revenue-share payments to Microsoft now "continue through 2030, independent of OpenAI's technology progress." Commentators read this as the end of the contractual AGI trigger, although OpenAI's announcement did not say what happens to the expert panel [s24].

Leading Candidates

OpenAI

OpenAI defines AGI as "a highly autonomous system that outperforms humans at most economically valuable work," and uses a five-level internal framework running from chatbots to AI that can do the work of an organization [s4]. Its current flagship, GPT-6 Astra, launched on 3 September 2026 with a focus on computer use. At launch it went only to enterprise customers [s20]. OpenAI rated it the first of its models to reach its "critical" cybersecurity capability threshold [s20]. A successor, GPT-6.1 Sol, was added to the ARC-AGI-3 leaderboard on 29 September [s22].

Astra's headline result was on ARC-AGI-3, a benchmark of interactive puzzle games. OpenAI reported 99.9% using a "souped-up" harness, the software around the model that keeps its reasoning state between steps. On ARC-AGI-3's standard harness it scored about 66% in OpenAI's figures and 62.7% in ARC Prize's own test [s20][s21]. ARC Prize now reports both conditions separately on its leaderboard [s21].

OpenAI's roadmap targets fully automated AI researchers by March 2028 [s5]. The company has restructured into a for-profit public benefit corporation and dropped the word "safely" from its mission statement [s13]. Altman's stated goal is an internal system he would call AGI by the end of 2026 [s23].

Anthropic

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei said at Davos in January 2026 that predictions of human-level AI by 2026–27 might not be "that far off" [s6][s16]. In September 2026 he published "We must pace the frontier," arguing that "we must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." He proposed third-party evaluators with employee-like access to frontier labs, and common limits on the rate of progress among democratic countries. He wrote that "pacing does not mean halting model training or technical progress." The essay does not use the term AGI or claim that current systems qualify [s29]. Anthropic has not published a formal AGI definition.

On 9 November 2026 Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 to the public and Claude Mythos 5.2 to vetted organizations, its first frontier releases since the pacing essay. A spokesperson said "we called for pacing, not pausing" [s30]. Claude Fable 5.2 holds the top standard-harness score on the official ARC-AGI-3 leaderboard at 81.2%, and 97.7% with the provider harness [s22]. The earlier Claude Opus 5 scored 30% on ARC-AGI-3 [s20].

Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis has shortened his timeline sharply during 2026. At Davos in January he said current systems were "nowhere near" human-level AGI and needed "one or two more breakthroughs" [s6][s16]. In a WSJ Magazine profile published on 20 October 2026, he said he expects AGI within two to three years: "It's soon … I'm updating nearer and nearer." He defines AGI as human-level cognition across all domains and tasks [s28].

Google's newest model, Gemini 4 Carbon, appeared on the ARC-AGI-3 leaderboard on 10 November 2026 with 65.1% on the standard harness and 99.1% with the provider harness [s22]. In spring 2026 DeepMind published a stricter test for AGI, described under "What Would Constitute AGI" below [s27].

Other Players

Elon Musk's xAI has made the boldest claims without evidence. Musk earlier predicted superhuman AI by 2026 [s9]. In September 2026 he posted that Grok 5 would be xAI's first AGI model. Grok 5 has no release date and no published benchmarks [s34]. xAI has not published a formal AGI definition.

Yann LeCun, who left Meta in late 2025, argues that language models alone will not reach AGI. His company, AMI Labs, builds "world models" that learn from physical reality, based on his JEPA architecture. In March 2026 it raised about $1.03 billion [s18][s31]. It had no public product as of this update.

What Would Constitute AGI

There is no agreed-upon definition of AGI. Major proposals differ significantly:

OpenAI's definition: "A highly autonomous system that outperforms humans at most economically valuable work." This is an economic framing — it ties AGI to labor market displacement rather than cognitive benchmarks [s4].

DeepMind's cognitive framework: DeepMind's 2023 "Levels of AGI" paper rated systems on both breadth and depth of ability [s8]. In 2026 it published "Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework," which splits general intelligence into ten cognitive faculties, including problem solving and social cognition. Under this test, a system must score above the median of a human sample on all ten. The authors argue that a weakness in any one faculty likely leaves some everyday human tasks out of reach [s27][s25]. A separate October 2025 paper, "A Definition of AGI" by Dan Hendrycks and colleagues, uses ten domains based on the Cattell-Horn-Carroll theory of human intelligence. It scored GPT-4 at 27% and GPT-5 at 57% of the way to AGI, with 0% for long-term memory storage in both [s33].

ARC-AGI benchmarks: François Chollet designed these tests of novel reasoning to be "easy for humans, hard for AI" [s11]. ARC-AGI-3, launched in 2026, uses interactive games that require exploration, planning and memory. In ARC Prize's study of 458 people, every game was beaten by at least two participants [s32]. In March 2026 the best AI score was below 1%. By November, Claude Fable 5.2 reached 81.2% on the standard harness, and several models passed 97% with harnesses that preserve the model's reasoning state [s22][s21]. In its post on Astra's result, ARC Prize said it was not claiming the model is AGI [s21]. ARC Prize 2026 results are due on 4 December 2026 [s22].

Turing-style functional tests: The February 2026 Nature commentary argues for inference to the best explanation — if a system produces outputs indistinguishable from a generally intelligent agent across diverse domains, it is generally intelligent. Critics counter that this conflates competence with performance and ignores underlying mechanisms [s2].

Functional/economic definitions: Sequoia Capital's January 2026 essay defines AGI as "the ability to figure things out," arguing that long-horizon agents already meet this bar. METR tracking data shows agent task performance doubling every seven months, projecting agents capable of tasks requiring a full human workday by 2028 [s12]. This pragmatic framing sidesteps the cognitive debates but is contested — as MIT Technology Review noted, achieving a one-hour time horizon on METR evaluations does not mean replacing one hour of real-world human work [s19].

A credible AGI declaration would likely need to satisfy multiple criteria: consistent expert-level performance across diverse domains (not just language), ability to learn novel tasks without retraining, robust reasoning under uncertainty, and independent verification by researchers outside the declaring organization. No current system has been shown to meet all of these criteria. Most frontier results in 2026, including Astra's, come from the labs' own reports or from a single benchmark [s20][s21].
